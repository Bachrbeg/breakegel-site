Kegel exercises for men: the complete guide

Updated November 10, 2026 · Reading time: 6 min

In short: Kegel exercises are not only for women. In men, they strengthen the pelvic floor, which helps control the bladder (especially after prostate surgery), reduces post-urination dribbling and improves erection and ejaculation control. Routine: 10 slow squeezes then 10 quick ones, 3 times a day, for at least 6 weeks.

Men have a pelvic floor too

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles stretched between the pubic bone and the tailbone. In men it supports the bladder and rectum, wraps around the urethra and plays a direct role in erection and ejaculation. Like any muscle, it gets stronger with training and weaker with age, inactivity, excess weight, chronic coughing or prostate surgery.

Kegel exercises for men simply consist of squeezing and releasing these muscles voluntarily and repeatedly.

Benefits of Kegel exercises for men

  • Bladder control: fewer leaks when you cough or lift, fewer urgent trips, and above all a faster return to continence after a prostatectomy. Urologists prescribe them almost systematically before and after that operation.
  • Post-urination dribbling: one firm squeeze after urinating empties the last segment of the urethra.
  • Sexual function: several clinical studies have shown improved erectile rigidity and better ejaculation control after 3 months of regular exercise, in a proportion of men.
  • Prevention: a toned pelvic floor limits the problems that appear with age.

How to find the right muscles

Two simple cues:

  1. While urinating, try to stop the stream: the muscles that tighten are the ones you are looking for. Do this test once to locate them, not as a regular exercise.
  2. Imagine you are holding back gas while drawing the penis inward. The testicles lift slightly; the lower belly stays relaxed.

Your glutes, thighs and abs should not move. If you are holding your breath, you are pushing too hard.

The routine

A session takes 2 to 4 minutes:

  1. 10 slow squeezes: hold for 3 seconds, relax for 3 seconds. Gradually work up to a 10-second hold.
  2. 10 quick squeezes: squeeze 1 second, relax 1 second.

Repeat the session 3 times a day (morning, midday, evening). Fully relaxing between squeezes matters as much as the squeeze itself. Sitting, standing or lying down all work; start lying down if that is easier.

How long until results?

The first effects on bladder control usually appear after 4 to 6 weeks of daily practice. For sexual function, allow about 3 months. Consistency matters more than intensity: three short sessions a day beat one long session a week.

After prostate surgery

After a prostatectomy the internal sphincter is often removed and the pelvic floor becomes the main guarantor of continence. Starting the exercises before surgery, then resuming as soon as the surgeon allows (usually once the catheter is removed), clearly speeds up recovery. A pelvic-floor physiotherapist can check that you are squeezing correctly.

Frequently asked questions

Do Kegel exercises work for men?

Yes. Urologists recommend them for continence after prostate surgery, and several clinical studies have shown benefits for erection and ejaculation control.

How many Kegels a day should a man do?

3 sessions a day of 10 slow squeezes then 10 quick ones, about 30 slow and 30 quick squeezes in total.

Do Kegel exercises help erections?

They can: the bulbocavernosus muscle, part of the pelvic floor, contributes to erectile rigidity. Improvement is often seen after 3 months of regular practice, but they do not replace medical advice for a persistent problem.

Can you do too many Kegels?

Yes. A permanently tight pelvic floor can become painful. Respect the relaxation time and do not exceed 3 to 5 sessions a day.

Important: this guide is informational and does not replace medical advice. If you have pain, significant leaks or any doubt, see a doctor, midwife or physiotherapist.
