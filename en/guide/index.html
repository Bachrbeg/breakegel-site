Kegel exercises: the complete beginner's guide

Updated October 24, 2026 · 6 min read

In short: Kegel exercises consist of squeezing and then relaxing the pelvic floor muscles. A typical session lasts 2 to 4 minutes: 10 slow squeezes of 3 to 10 seconds, then 10 quick squeezes, repeated 3 times a day. The first results usually appear after 4 to 6 weeks of regular practice.

What is a Kegel exercise?

A Kegel exercise is a voluntary contraction of the pelvic floor muscles, a group of muscles stretched like a hammock between the pubic bone and the tailbone. They support the bladder, the bowel and, in women, the uterus. The exercises are named after Arnold Kegel, the American gynecologist who described them in the 1940s.

They are for men and women alike. Women are often advised to do them after pregnancy or around menopause. Men benefit after prostate surgery or to improve urinary and sexual control.

What are they for?

  • Preventing or reducing urine leaks when coughing, laughing or exercising.
  • Supporting the pelvic organs and lowering the risk of prolapse.
  • Speeding up recovery after childbirth or prostate surgery.
  • Improving sexual sensation and control, for men and women.

How to find the right muscles

The simplest way: imagine you are holding back urine or gas. The muscles that tighten are your pelvic floor. You should feel a gentle lift inside, without moving your buttocks, thighs or belly.

Important: don't practice while actually urinating, except once to locate the muscles. Regularly stopping the flow can interfere with bladder emptying.

How to do a session, step by step

  1. Get comfortable sitting, lying down or standing. Breathe normally, shoulders and belly relaxed.
  2. Squeeze the pelvic floor, as if holding back, for 3 seconds to start.
  3. Relax completely for the same amount of time. Relaxing matters as much as squeezing.
  4. Repeat 10 times. That is your set of slow squeezes.
  5. Follow with 10 quick squeezes: tighten hard for one second, release for two. These train the reflex you need when you cough or sneeze.

Once 3-second squeezes feel easy, move up to 5, then 7, then 10 seconds. This is exactly how the levels in the Break app progress.

How many times a day?

The most common recommendation is 3 sessions a day, for example morning, midday and evening. Doing more doesn't speed up results and can tire the muscles. Three sessions every day beat ten sessions every other day.

When will you see results?

Like any muscle, the pelvic floor strengthens gradually. Most people notice an improvement after 4 to 6 weeks of daily practice, with clear results after 3 months. After that, a maintenance routine keeps the gains.

Common mistakes

  • Holding your breath. Keep breathing normally during the squeeze.
  • Tightening the buttocks, thighs or abs instead of the pelvic floor.
  • Pushing down instead of lifting up and in.
  • Forgetting to relax. A muscle that is always tense doesn't get stronger.
  • Stopping too early. Consistency over several weeks makes all the difference.

Frequently asked questions

Are Kegel exercises only for women?

No. Men have the same pelvic floor and get the same benefits, especially for urinary and sexual control.

Can I do them anywhere?

Yes. Sitting at a desk, on the bus or lying down, nobody can tell. That's why Break uses neutral notification text and vibrations.

Do they hurt?

No. A correct squeeze is painless. If you feel pain, stop and see a doctor or a pelvic floor physiotherapist.

How long is a session?

Between 2 and 4 minutes depending on squeeze length. In Break, a level 1 session takes about 2 minutes.

Important: this guide is informational and does not replace medical advice. If you have pain, significant leaks or any doubt, see a doctor, midwife or physiotherapist.
