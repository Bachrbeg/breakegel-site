Urine leaks and Kegel exercises: what actually works

Updated November 10, 2026 · Reading time: 6 min

In short: for stress leaks (coughing, laughing, sport, lifting), strengthening the pelvic floor with Kegel exercises is the first-line treatment recommended by medical guidelines, with a high improvement rate after 3 months. For urgency (sudden strong urges), they also help, combined with bladder training.

The two main types of leaks

  • Stress incontinence: a few drops when abdominal pressure rises (coughing, sneezing, laughing, jumping, lifting). Cause: a weakened pelvic floor or sphincter. The most common form in women after pregnancy or around menopause, and in men after prostate surgery.
  • Urge incontinence: a sudden, compelling urge, sometimes without making it to the toilet. Cause: an "overactive" bladder.

Many people have a mixed form. In both cases, Kegel exercises are part of the treatment.

Why Kegels work

A toned pelvic floor closes the urethra at the right moment: just before a cough or an effort, a reflex squeeze prevents the leak. Training strengthens the muscle but, above all, relearns that reflex ("the knack": squeezing deliberately before coughing or lifting). For urgency, a strong, held squeeze during the urge inhibits the bladder contraction and buys time to reach the toilet.

International guidelines (ICS, NICE and others) place pelvic-floor muscle training first, before any medication or surgery, for stress and mixed incontinence.

The routine for leaks

  1. 3 sessions a day: 10 slow squeezes (3- to 10-second hold depending on level) then 10 quick ones.
  2. The knack: get into the habit of squeezing just before you cough, sneeze, laugh or lift.
  3. For urgency: when the urge hits, stop, squeeze hard for 5 to 10 seconds, breathe, wait for the urge to fade, then walk calmly to the toilet. Gradually space out your bathroom trips.
  4. For at least 3 months, then daily maintenance.

Expected results

In pelvic-floor rehabilitation studies, most women with stress incontinence report clear improvement or cure after 3 months of supervised exercise, and the effect lasts as long as the exercises continue. The first changes usually show between 4 and 6 weeks. In men after prostatectomy, the exercises bring continence back several weeks sooner.

Measures that help on top

  • Cut down on caffeine, alcohol and fizzy drinks, which irritate the bladder.
  • Don't restrict water: concentrated urine makes urgency worse.
  • Treat constipation and chronic cough, which strain the pelvic floor.
  • Lose weight if needed: every kilo less reduces pressure on the bladder.

When to see a doctor

See a doctor if the leaks started suddenly, come with pain, blood in the urine or fever, persist after 3 months of regular exercise, or have a major impact on your life. A pelvic-floor physiotherapist or specialised midwife can check your technique and offer biofeedback or electrical stimulation.

Frequently asked questions

Are Kegel exercises enough to cure urine leaks?

For mild to moderate stress incontinence, often yes: most people see clear improvement after 3 months. Severe cases may need other treatments in addition.

How long before the leaks decrease?

Usually 4 to 6 weeks for the first effects, 3 months for a clear result.

Do Kegels help with urgency too?

Yes. A strong, held squeeze during the urge inhibits the bladder contraction. Combine them with gradual bladder training.

Can I do Kegels with a urinary tract infection?

Wait until the infection is over: squeezing is uncomfortable and treatment comes first, with your doctor.

Important: this guide is informational and does not replace medical advice. If you have pain, significant leaks or any doubt, see a doctor, midwife or physiotherapist.
