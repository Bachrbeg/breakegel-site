Kegel exercises after childbirth

Updated November 10, 2026 · Reading time: 6 min

In short: after a vaginal birth or a C-section, the pelvic floor has been stretched or strained. Kegel exercises can resume gently within the first days if there is no pain, then intensify after the postnatal check-up (6 to 8 weeks). They complement, but do not replace, pelvic-floor physiotherapy with a midwife or physiotherapist.

What pregnancy and birth do to the pelvic floor

For nine months the pelvic floor carries the weight of the uterus, the baby and the amniotic fluid, under hormones that soften the tissues. A vaginal birth then stretches it strongly, sometimes with a tear or an episiotomy. Even after a C-section, the pregnancy itself has weakened these muscles.

A common result in the following weeks: urine leaks when laughing, coughing or carrying the baby, a feeling of heaviness, and reduced sensation. These problems are very common and, in the vast majority of cases, reversible.

When to start

  • The first days: very gentle, pain-free squeezes help restore circulation and awareness of the pelvic floor. If a tear, an episiotomy or stitches hurt, wait.
  • Until the postnatal check-up (6 to 8 weeks): short sessions, 3-second squeezes, no straining. Avoid heavy lifting and classic sit-ups.
  • After medical clearance: the full routine, plus pelvic-floor physiotherapy if prescribed.

When in doubt, ask your midwife: every birth is different.

How to do them, step by step

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, breathing calmly.
  2. As you exhale, squeeze gently as if holding back urine and gas at the same time, lifting inward. Belly, buttocks and thighs stay relaxed.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then release completely for 3 seconds.
  4. Repeat 10 times, then do 10 quick one-second squeezes.

Gradually lengthen the hold to 10 seconds and move to sitting, then standing, which are more demanding.

How often, and for how long?

Aim for 3 sessions a day of 2 to 4 minutes, for at least 3 months. Stress leaks usually decrease after 4 to 6 weeks. After that, one or two sessions a day are enough for maintenance, and many women continue for life, especially around menopause.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Bearing down instead of lifting up: the opposite of what you want.
  • Holding your breath or tightening your abdomen.
  • Going back to running or sit-ups before the pelvic floor has regained its tone.
  • Waiting for it to "go away on its own": the earlier you start, the faster the recovery.

Kegels and pelvic-floor physiotherapy work together

Physiotherapy with a midwife or a pelvic-floor physio checks that your squeeze is correct, uses biofeedback or electrical stimulation if needed, and corrects mistakes. Kegel exercises at home, between and after the sessions, are what make the results last. Frequently asked questions

Can I do Kegel exercises right after giving birth?

Yes, gently and without pain, from the first days if you feel up to it. If you have stitches, a tear or pain, wait for healing and ask your midwife.

Do I still need Kegels after a C-section?

Yes. The pregnancy itself has weakened the pelvic floor, even without a vaginal birth.

How long does it take to regain a strong pelvic floor?

Stress leaks usually decrease after 4 to 6 weeks of daily exercise, with good results after 3 months.

Do Kegels replace pelvic-floor physiotherapy?

No. They complement it. Physiotherapy checks technique and treats harder cases; daily Kegels maintain the result.

Important: this guide is informational and does not replace medical advice. If you have pain, significant leaks or any doubt, see a doctor, midwife or physiotherapist.
