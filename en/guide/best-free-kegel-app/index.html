Which app should you use for Kegel exercises?

Updated November 10, 2026 · Reading time: 5 min

In short: a Kegel app does two things: remind you to do your sessions and guide you through every squeeze. What matters: neutral notification text, vibration or sound guidance that works with the phone locked, progressive levels, simple tracking, and respect for your privacy. Break ticks those boxes for free on iPhone.

What is a Kegel app actually for?

The exercises themselves are simple and need no equipment. What makes most people fail is forgetting (sessions leave no trace in your day) and pacing (counting 3, 5 or 10 seconds in your head, 20 times in a row, three times a day, gets old fast). A good app solves exactly those two problems, and nothing more is needed.

The criteria that matter

  • Discreet reminders: the notification text must not reveal what it's about to someone glancing at your screen. "Take 2 min" is enough.
  • Guidance that works with the screen locked: sounds or voice, so you can do the session on the train or at your desk without looking at the phone.
  • Vibrations synced with the squeeze when the phone is in your hand.
  • Progression: hold time should increase over time (3 → 5 → 7 → 10 seconds), ideally automatically.
  • Simple tracking: a calendar with successful days and the current streak. No need for complex charts.
  • Privacy: this is an intimate topic. An app that requires an account, collects data or shows ads has no place here.
  • Price: the exercises have been free since the 1940s; a subscription isn't justified for a timer.
